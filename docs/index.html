Let’s start here

AI sounds new.
Some of it is
already familiar.

Artificial intelligence is a broad name for computer systems that do tasks such as recognizing patterns, making predictions or generating content.

You may have met it in email spam filters or video recommendations. A chatbot is another way to use it: you type or speak, and it replies.

A useful distinction

Sorting things
vs. making things

AI that spots patterns

A spam filter looks for signs that a message might be junk. It makes a prediction—and can get it wrong.

AI that creates something

Generative AI produces new text, pictures or audio using patterns learned during training. Ask for a birthday poem and it creates one, rather than simply finding an existing poem.

Does a chatbot understand like a person?

It can follow instructions and produce helpful explanations. But fluent conversation is not proof of human understanding, feelings or accuracy. Check facts that matter.

A bridge between generations

“What my grandkids mean by…”

You don’t have to know every new word to join in. Open a phrase, then use the conversation starter to explore it together.

“I asked a chatbot.”

A chatbot is a tool you can have a written or spoken conversation with. Some generate replies using AI. A friendly reply doesn’t mean there is a person on the other end.

Ask them: “What do you find it useful for? Can you show me?”

“You need a better prompt.”

A prompt is the request or information you give an AI. Saying what you need, what matters and how you want the answer can help it respond usefully.

Try together: Ask for a birthday message, then ask for a funnier version.

“That picture is AI-generated.”

AI made or changed the picture. It may look like a photograph of something that never happened. Looks alone may not reveal whether it’s real.

Ask them: “Where did it come from? Can we find the original?”

“The AI hallucinated.”

That means it generated false or unsupported information. It might invent a fact or a source while sounding completely confident.

Ask them: “How did you check the answer?”

“My algorithm keeps showing me this.”

An algorithm is a set of steps a computer follows. Here, they mean a recommendation system choosing what appears in their feed. Not every algorithm is AI.

Ask them: “Do you think we see the same news and videos?”

“That’s a deepfake.”

AI-generated or altered audio, images or video can make a real person appear to say or do something they didn’t. Some are entertainment; others are deceptive.

Try together: Agree to verify unusual requests using a number you already know.

Family, friends, neighbours—learning together can start with anyone.

Confidence includes knowing when to pause

A familiar voice.
An unfamiliar request.

Scams can use convincing messages, fake caller ID and AI-generated voices. You don’t need to prove how a message was made to stop and check it.

  1. Pause. Don’t send money or information under pressure.
  2. Check. Contact the person through a number you already have.
  3. Decide. Wait until you have confirmed what’s happening.

What if I’ve already sent money or shared details?

Contact your financial institution promptly using its official number. Save the messages and payment details, and report the incident to local police and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. You deserve support; these scams are designed to be convincing.
