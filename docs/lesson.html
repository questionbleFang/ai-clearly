Behind the lesson

Designed for useful confidence—not fear

This prototype is for older adults who may be curious about AI but do not want technical jargon. It uses large type, concrete examples, immediate feedback, and repetition of safety-critical actions.

Measurable learning outcomes

After the lesson, a learner should be able to:

  1. Describe a chatbot as a tool that generates likely responses, not a person or truth machine.
  2. Name two useful, low-risk tasks for AI.
  3. Use the Pause → Check → Decide routine before acting on important AI output.
  4. Recognize an urgent family-money request as a reason to verify through a known contact.
  5. Keep identifying, financial, and detailed health information out of casual chatbot prompts.

Evidence base

  • Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: Emergency and grandparent fraud
  • Privacy Commissioner of Canada: Privacy-protective generative AI principles
  • World Health Organization: Guidance on generative AI for health
  • NIST: Generative AI risk management profile
  • Bender et al.: “On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots”
